# System Requirements & Prototype Architectural Scope

## Overview
This document specifies the initial functional requirements and architectural interpretation for the **Enterprise Supply Chain Demand Forecasting & Inventory Optimization Platform**. The target system is designed to simulate an enterprise-scale supply chain intelligence platform at prototype scale. By integrating four diverse public supply chain benchmark datasets—**Brazilian E-Commerce by Olist**, **DataCo Smart Supply Chain**, **Kaggle Rossmann Store Sales**, and **M5 Forecasting (Walmart)**—the prototype models realistic enterprise logistics, demand dynamics, and inventory risks without requiring full enterprise live streaming infrastructure.

---

## Core Modules & Prototype Interpretation

### 1. Enterprise Data Pipeline
The **Enterprise Data Pipeline** module is responsible for ingesting, cleaning, and schema-standardizing multi-source supply chain datasets into a unified relational data store. At prototype scale, this module transitions from continuous live-streaming enterprise ingestion (such as Kafka or SAP ERP webhooks) to a structured batch ETL pipeline utilizing SQLite/PostgreSQL and Python data processing tools. The pipeline ingests static historical records across the four benchmark datasets, standardizing heterogeneous domain entities—such as customer e-commerce orders and delivery transit times from **Olist**, fulfillment status and late delivery flags from **DataCo**, daily store-level retail sales and promotional calendars from **Rossmann**, and fine-grained product category/SKU sales histories from **M5**—into a consolidated relational schema (storing harmonized timestamps, store/warehouse identifiers, product master attributes, lead times, and promotional flags) ready for feature extraction and modeling.

### 2. Feature Engineering
The **Feature Engineering** module converts raw historical tables into structured time-series and operational features required for predictive modeling and risk analytics across the supply chain network. At prototype scale, the automated feature generation pipeline synthesizes time-series lag variables and rolling window statistics (e.g., 7-day, 14-day, and 30-day moving averages of sales velocity and demand volatility) primarily derived from **M5** product sales and **Rossmann** store transactions. Additionally, it computes seasonality indices, promotional impact flags, state/school holiday calendar indicators, and supplier lead time distributions and reliability scores extracted from historical delivery performance in **DataCo** and fulfillment durations in **Olist**, producing a feature store that feeds both forecasting algorithms and downstream inventory decision logic.

### 3. Demand Forecasting Engine
The **Demand Forecasting Engine** provides multi-horizon predictive demand analytics across product categories, store locations, and individual SKUs to enable proactive procurement and distribution planning. At prototype scale, the engine trains supervised gradient boosted decision trees (**LightGBM**, **XGBoost**) and statistical time-series models (**Prophet**) on historical store and item sales from **Rossmann** and **M5**. The engine outputs daily, weekly, and monthly demand projections along with confidence intervals, capturing seasonal peaks, holiday lift effects, and regional purchasing patterns to drive downstream safety stock calculations and replenishment schedules.

### 4. Inventory Optimization Engine
The **Inventory Optimization Engine** computes mathematical inventory targets and replenishment policies to balance product availability against inventory holding costs across fulfillment nodes. At prototype scale, the engine applies classical inventory optimization formulations—computing **Safety Stock**, **Reorder Points (ROP)**, and **Economic Order Quantity (EOQ)**—per SKU and warehouse location by combining demand forecast distributions (from **M5** and **Rossmann**) with lead time variability and cost structures derived from **DataCo** and **Olist**. The module generates actionable procurement recommendations, optimal stock allocation targets, and inter-warehouse transfer suggestions to minimize stockout risks while avoiding excessive capital tie-up.

### 5. Supply Chain Risk Engine
The **Supply Chain Risk Engine** identifies, quantifies, and flags operational vulnerabilities across the supply chain network prior to fulfillment failure. At prototype scale, this engine utilizes risk scoring models and anomaly detection logic trained on **DataCo** delivery risk flags (such as late delivery risk and shipping delays) and **Olist** transit duration distributions, cross-referenced with demand volatility metrics from **M5** and **Rossmann**. The engine evaluates nodes and SKUs for supplier delay risk, stockout exposure, overstock risk, slow-moving inventory, dead stock, and unusual demand spikes, categorizing risk severity levels to empower supply chain managers to take preventative action.

### 6. Scenario Simulation
The **Scenario Simulation** engine offers interactive "what-if" modeling capabilities enabling supply chain planners to test stress-test operational strategies and evaluate disruption impacts before real-world execution. At prototype scale, the simulation module exposes parameterized controls allowing users to adjust variables such as supplier delay shocks (modeled after **DataCo** lead-time distributions), promotional price changes and demand surges (inspired by **M5** price elasticity and **Rossmann** promo schedules), and holiday sales spikes. The engine dynamically recalculates projected inventory trajectories, fill rates, and stockout probabilities, allowing users to compare baseline forecasts against stress-test outcomes.

### 7. Executive Dashboard
The **Executive Dashboard** provides an intuitive, web-based visualization UI (built with Streamlit / React & FastAPI) that aggregates top-level supply chain KPIs and operational metrics into decision-ready visual scorecards. At prototype scale, the dashboard synthesizes data outputs from all four benchmark dataset pipelines to display critical performance metrics including Forecast Accuracy (WAPE, MAE, RMSE), Inventory Health status, Fill Rates, estimated Warehouse Utilization, Stockout Probabilities, and automated Procurement Recommendations. Interactive filters enable stakeholders to drill down by region, product category, store location, and planning horizon.

### 8. Alert Center
The **Alert Center** acts as an automated monitoring and notification mechanism that scans inventory thresholds, risk engine outputs, and model health metrics to surface prioritized operational warnings. At prototype scale, the center monitors safety stock breaches, impending overstock conditions, demand spike anomalies, predicted supplier delivery delays, warehouse capacity limits, and forecast model drift. Automated alerts are generated and classified by severity (Critical, High, Medium, Info) and displayed within a dedicated alert feed and API endpoint, facilitating rapid operational intervention.

### 9. ML / MLOps Components
The **ML / MLOps Components** establish a standardized framework for machine learning model lifecycle management, experiment tracking, model registry, and governance. At prototype scale, this module leverages **MLflow** to log experiment runs, hyperparameter tuning results (via Bayesian optimization), feature selection outputs, and model artifacts for forecasting models trained on **Rossmann** and **M5** data. The module incorporates time-series cross-validation splitters, automated retraining hooks, and data drift detection mechanisms that monitor input feature distributions and forecast error degradation over time.
